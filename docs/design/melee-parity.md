## Deviations from Melee

Each row Tom decided to keep different from Melee, with his reason and date. These rows are the only place a
deviation's reason, issue and decision date are written.

- SDI travel bound `sdi-bound`: keeps SDI choices useful while bounding visible jumps and repeated-hit travel (docs/gameplay-design.md, Bounded SDI) (Tom 2026-10-06, #70)
  - Ours: 3 a frame, 12 a hit with ASDI, 24 a string; Melee: none
  - Other games: Weakened in later games ([SmashWiki](https://www.ssbwiki.com/Smash_directional_influence))
- Frames a full charge can be held `smash-charge-hold`: a held smash becomes a timing mix-up, one rule for every fighter (Tom 2026-10-26, #328)
  - Ours: 120; Melee: 0
  - Other games: Ultimate: the same 60 frames of charge, then up to 120 more frames held at full charge (180 in all) that add no damage ([SmashWiki: Smash attack](https://www.ssbwiki.com/Smash_attack), [Charge](https://www.ssbwiki.com/Charge))
- Stale-move negation `staling`: move diversity comes from move design, not a repeat penalty (Tom 2026-10-04)
  - Ours: none; Melee: 9-move queue
  - Other games: Ultimate keeps the 9-move queue, adds a freshness bonus, counts shield hits and weakens the knockback effect ([SmashWiki](https://www.ssbwiki.com/Stale-move_negation))
- Clank and priority `clank`: simultaneous strikes resolve symmetrically as trades, with no unseen priority system (docs/gameplay-design.md, Same-frame strikes and clanking) (Tom 2026-10-06)
  - Ours: strikes trade; only grabs clash; Melee: ground attacks within 9% clank; aerials do not clank
- Missed tech into the floor at speed `floor-rebound`: a missed tech keeps its momentum, so a hard spike can carry off the top (docs/physics.md, Floor bounces) (Tom 2026-10-15)
  - Ours: rebounds ×0.8, as off walls; Melee: the fall stops
  - Other games: Smash 4 and Ultimate: floors no longer halt knockback momentum; a missed tech rebounds like a stage spike, so a meteor can KO off the top ([SmashWiki](https://www.ssbwiki.com/Floor))
- Shieldstun from an aerial on a full shield, probed at damage 1-50 `shieldstun-aerial`: aerials take a third more shieldstun per damage, tilting slightly toward aggression (docs/gameplay-design.md, Aerials on shield) (Tom 2026-10-06, #106)
  - Ours: ⌊(0.6d + 2)·200/201⌋; Melee: ⌊(0.45d + 2)·200/201⌋
  - Other games: Ultimate: ⌊0.8 × damage × 0.33 + 2⌋ for aerials, 0.264 a damage, with shield hitlag × 0.67 ([SmashWiki](https://www.ssbwiki.com/Shieldstun), [hitlag](https://www.ssbwiki.com/Hitlag)). Rivals 2: 0.8 × damage + 1 for every attack, and the input buffer drops to 2 frames after a shield contact ([Dragdown](https://dragdown.wiki/wiki/RoA2/System_Mechanics/Defense))
- Shield release lag `shield-release`: Melee's 15 makes dropping shield almost never worth it; Ultimate's 11, the starting point, makes it a real option beside jumping or rolling out (docs/gameplay-design.md, Principles) (Tom 2026-10-06, #100)
  - Ours: 11; Melee: 15
  - Other games: Ultimate: 11 frames
- Powershield `powershield`: a true parry whose reward is the defender's own punish (docs/gameplay-design.md, Powershield and parry) (Tom 2026-10-06, #102)
  - Ours: a parry: no shield damage, no shieldstun, no release lag; Melee: first 4 frames of shield on a hit: no shield damage, more pushback, same shieldstun; first 2 reflect projectiles
  - Other games: Ultimate: release-timed in the first 5 frames of the 11-frame shield drop; any attack skips the drop lag and acts 3 frames sooner than a block against direct hits; no reflection ([SmashWiki](https://www.ssbwiki.com/Perfect_shield)). Rivals 2: a 4-frame perfect shield plus a separate parry, active 6–13, that stuns the attacker 40–100 frames ([Dragdown](https://dragdown.wiki/wiki/RoA2/System_Mechanics/Defense))
- Shield drop through a platform `platform-shield-drop`: shielded fighters keep their jump, dodge or release choices (docs/gameplay-design.md, Platform shield drop) (Tom 2026-10-14, #103)
  - Ours: none: a raised shield stays on the platform; Melee: down while shielding drops through
  - Other games: Removed in Ultimate; Project+ keeps it
- Grab hold frames, probed at 0-300% `grab-hold`: Ultimate's hold formula with a visible escape meter (docs/gameplay-design.md, Grab holds and pummels) (Tom 2026-10-14, #101)
  - Ours: ⌊90 + 1.75p⌋; Melee: ⌊76 + 1.6p⌋
  - Other games: Brawl onward: 90 + 1.7 frames per percent, 8 per stick mash input (14.4 per button in Smash 4 and Ultimate), never under 19 ([SmashWiki](https://www.ssbwiki.com/Grab)). - Teeter at an edge, and the teeter cancel `teeter`: Melee's teeter is one forward-facing animation; a symmetric rule leaves no hidden facing rule deciding the result (docs/gameplay-design.md, Teeter and the teeter cancel) (Tom 2026-10-26, #458)
