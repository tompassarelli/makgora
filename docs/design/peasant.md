# Peon

The Orc Peon is a stubborn worker who wins by making an awkward little worksite,
then landing a clean axe blow when the opponent dismantles it. His short arms,
slow run and committed throws make being rushed down costly. All numbers below
are original provisional Smashcraft tuning; the references supply identity and
trade-offs, never copied hitboxes or animation.

## Sources and body

[Liquipedia's Peon](https://liquipedia.net/warcraft/Peon) documents harvesting,
Repair, Pillage and sheltering in a Burrow. Blizzard's
[Peon page](https://classic.battle.net/war3/orc/units/peon.shtml) and
[Orc basics](https://classic.battle.net/war3/orc/basics.shtml) describe the same
worker and the repair/retreat exchange around a Burrow. The ordinary Warcraft
axe, carried lumber, work gestures and the base game's Peon voice set provide
the character: “Work work”, “Zug zug”, and “Me not that kind of orc”.

The named body counterpart is
[Ultimate Villager](https://www.ssbwiki.com/Villager_(SSBU)#Stats): weight 92,
run 1.397 and air speed 0.987. Smashcraft's reference weight is 75, run is 2.2
Smash units and air speed is 1.0. Therefore Peon's weight and air multipliers are 92/75
and 0.987. His run is 1.6/2.2, faster than Villager's 1.397, because at 1.397 the
seeded field left him unable to reach his tool range (#227). Width 0.88 and height 0.80 fit the stock worker; jumps,
gravity and shared dodge windows retain the roster defaults.

[Villager's kit](https://www.ssbwiki.com/Villager_(SSBU)#Moveset) supplies the
ordinary-tool joke and committed zoning shape.
[Timber](https://www.ssbwiki.com/Timber) supplies the axe payoff;
[Lloid Rocket](https://www.ssbwiki.com/Lloid_Rocket) supplies slow, visible
pressure that can be challenged before release;
[Pocket](https://www.ssbwiki.com/Pocket) supplies a brief defensive read.
[Mr. Game & Watch's tools](https://www.ssbwiki.com/Mr._Game_%26_Watch_(SSBU)#Moveset)
supply distinct silhouettes for mundane jobs turned into attacks.

Hands remain inside exposed limb capsules; the axe blade alone is disjoint.
Every tilt angle, smash, axe aerial, get-up and ledge attack keeps each active
frame's region on the drawn axe (ts/test/peon-axe.test.ts, #520).
Get-up and ledge attacks are Peon-local moves with the template's shared timing
and hit effects, their regions fitted to the drawn axe: get-up reaches 47 ahead,
ledge attack 41. No throw is designed as an inescapable loop: up throw permits a short
juggle, down throw offers the victim a tech direction, and shared throw-hitstun
rules refuse immediate regrabs.

## Matchup identity

Rifleman outrange the worker but must respect a built worksite.
Illidan and Warden outmaneuver his slow run and punish failed Repair reads.
Blademaster's sword and Forsaken Paladin's sword beat his short tools directly.
Mountain King wins close heavy trades; Peon instead makes him approach lumber.
Lich controls broader zones; Peon has no zone of his own, only a log that reaches mid range.
Dreadlord chases and grabs more effectively; Peon commits to stationary space.
Shadow Hunter's mobile returning glaive differs from Peon's one-way lumber.
Pit Lord owns wider melee arcs but gives Peon more body to poke.
Beastmaster commands mobile companions; Peon has none and fights alone.
Lich King earns stronger stored finishers; Pillage only replenishes ordinary mana.

The CPU keeps a 0–75-unit gap for tools and grabs, favors running into close
pressure, and heaves the log from mid range, 70–130 units out, where its tipper lands. It follows
hits with tool strikes and aerials, uses Repair against visible incoming hits, and saves its jump before
Worksite Launch. Base-game Peon model, lumber, missiles, portraits and sounds
are the only art sources. Authored motion and private pooled clips preserve
recognizable work gestures in both facings at gameplay zoom.

## Play-style profile

Tom-tunable draft (8 Oct; smashcraft:docs/design/balance.md, "Play-style profiles"). A stubborn worker who wins by making an awkward little worksite.

```balance-profile
fighter: peon
archetype: grappler
aerials: nair 15-45, fair 10-40, bair 10-40, uair 5-30, dair 5-30
air-share: 10-40
approach: 50-80
ranged: 0-25
specials: neutral 2-12, side 2-12, up 0-8, down 2-12
envelope: special.down.ground endLag Rest's 225-frame sleep is the price of its frame-2 kill hit (#563 finding 13)
envelope: special.down.ground.ex endLag EX Rest keeps the same 225-frame sleep
```

Path draft for Tom to correct (#358):

```ceiling-plan
fighter: peon
path: decision
basic-moves: forward-tilt,down-tilt,neutral-air
```

Basic gameplan: Use tool pokes and a neutral aerial. Keep one approach, use the listed moves, no read-based punishes or advanced kit sequences. Recovery keeps the normal up special.

Expert gameplan: Choose tools, building pressure, grabs and deceptive approaches. Use the full authored kit and gameplan at the same CPU skill.

## Signature strong/weak move (#389)

Forward smash, split by position: strong hit sweetspot, frames 22-24, 20%; weak hit sourspot, frames 22-24, 14.3%. The pick bites hardest at its point, like a swing at the mine.
