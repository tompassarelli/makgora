# View

- Player view: `bun wisp view scene DATA_DIR...` and `bun wisp view frame
  FRAME.ppm...` report what a player would see wrong; `view models --prune` removes facts for deleted models without remeasurement; `view models --check` refuses, at landing, when a scene model has no facts; `view models` rewrites
  the model facts they read; `view models ... --only MODEL,...` refreshes only the named rows (smashcraft:docs/player-view.md); `view strikes --assets DIR`
  rewrites the hero strike moments swings and specials align to
  (smashcraft:docs/fighter-animation-work.md, "Hero swing alignment"); `view strikes --check`
  fails, naming the fighters, when the committed table differs from what the checkout's models measure;
  `view motion --assets DIR [--character ID]` measures every fighter's movement and recovery
  clips and rewrites their foot cadence and audit (smashcraft:docs/fighter-motion.md);
  `view reach --assets DIR [--character ID]` rewrites how far fighters' swings
  draw toward their strikes (smashcraft:docs/hurtboxes.md);
  `view reachcues --assets DIR` rewrites which normals show a reach flare: an
  active hit region more than 0.15 body heights (132 u) past the drawn Classic
  body and weapon swept over the active frames (#481);
  `view cues [--move FIGHTER:MOVE]...` renders
  every attack and special cue in Wisp and fails any move over the effect budget
  (smashcraft:docs/design/visual-quality.md, "Effect budget").
- Classic effect visibility: `cd ts && bun scripts/wisp/effectVisibility.ts [--storage WC3_INSTALL] [--json] [--strict]`
  reads every stock model and texture from the Warcraft III 3.0.0.24268 install's storage (default `WC3_STORAGE`, else the Steam install), extracting what no render has cached and refusing any file the extractor cannot read, with imported assets from the frozen map inputs. The record names the build by its version and Build Key, so it does not depend on which renders filled the private cache. It reports every fighter's model-data visibility as visible, invisible or Unknown; `--json` includes every use, source digest and input hashes. `--strict` refuses invisible rows while listing Unknown rows and their reasons. Model-data visibility is separate from physical native acceptance.
  Animated translation, rotation and node flags do not make geometry unknown by themselves. Step and linear scale tracks are supported while their values stay nonzero; nonlinear, zero-crossing or otherwise unmodeled scale remains Unknown.
  Calibration (`EFFECT_CALIBRATION` in effectVisibility.ts): both commands refuse unless the judge flags the 0.0.145 Breath of Fire (#365) and frozen Shockwave (#484) uses for their own causes and passes the known-visible set (Cairne's playing Shockwave wave and four Blademaster effects seen in native Classic 3.0.0.24268 captures). A new reference needs Tom's playtest report or a native capture.
  Record the same evidence through `bun scripts/cueModels.ts --record [--storage WC3_INSTALL]`; the existing cue-record landing check validates its per-use/source/provenance digests without private assets on hosted runners, refuses missing/invisible uses and lists every Unknown reason. This is a model-data report check. Its success leaves every special/ultimate without native screen evidence unproven, including model-visible rows. Historical frozen Shockwave remains incomplete even with a geoset; a playing model alone still cannot close visibility. Confirmed emitter births and their bounded phase handoff require native active-window coverage (#365/#496).

Native Classic footprint/duration acceptance for #496 is `bun scripts/cueModels.ts --native --screen-captures PACK.json`. It retains the model checks and refuses any missing, stale, invisible or unproven native row. `bun scripts/classicEffectScreen.ts --captures PACK.json --out REPORT.json` produces the native screen measurements directly from capture pixels and enforces the same acceptance. Without captures it enumerates every fighter's four specials and ultimate, plus a separate row for every projectile special/ultimate use. Every projectile row needs a contiguous sample of its full lifetime, including mid-flight; one cast flash cannot establish its travelling art. Use `--report` only to enumerate pending work; it prints unproven rows and never supplies a visibility pass. Numeric acceptance remains pending until real calibration exists. Motion strips, hit-region agreement and the native cold-reader check in AGENTS.md remain additional visual acceptance.

Calibration positives, designated by Tom's 14 October playtest follow-up, are Blademaster neutral/side/up/down specials and ultimate. Negative controls are the 0.0.192 cues the playtest reported as unseen: Shadow Hunter's Spirit Glaive and Lich's Death and Decay and down special. The other 0.0.192 flags (Chen Breath of Fire, Mrgl & Grgl Belly Slide/Tidal Surf, Mountain King Thunder Clap, Thrall's slam, Cairne Shockwave) asked for clearer or better art, and native captures (#454, 2026-10-29) measured them at 7k to 100k pixels for up to 18 frames, larger than the smallest Blademaster positive, so no footprint threshold can treat them as invisible; a negative's `version` is `0.0.192`, with semver build metadata naming any input substitution (`0.0.192+art-2026-10-26` keeps 0.0.192's source and swaps only art inputs). The tool derives the pixel threshold from the smallest positive peak, then the duration threshold from the shortest consecutive positive run at that pixel threshold. Every negative must fall below that area/duration pair; overlap refuses calibration. No guessed pixel area or duration substitutes for those captures. Differences greater than 24 in a color channel use the existing native silhouette comparison's noise threshold inside the effect region; outside it only differences greater than 96 count as contamination, because Sky Deck's water animates in real time and shows scattered 24-level differences between any two captures; footprint and duration require the named calibration above.

Capture packs are private JSON with a `samples` array. Each sample names `move` (an exact row identifier printed by the report), `role` (`positive`, `negative` or `candidate`), `engine: "native-classic"`, `camera: "normal-match"`, `stage: "sky-deck"` (stage 0), the current `source` digest, `version`, `manifest`, `map`, `mapSha256`, inclusive `first`/`last` held match frames, and ordered `frames`. Each frame names its `frame`, three distinct capture references (`shown`, `off`, `repeatedOff`), an effect `region`, a `body` exclusion and `excluded` HUD/name/stamp rectangles. Boxes are `{x,y,width,height}` in captured-image pixels. A sample captured with `--crop` carries that `crop` box (its image size); the collector read the held frame's stamp from the whole screen before cropping, so the receipt's record stands in for the stamp, which the cropped image no longer shows. A reference is `{receipt,fixture,phase,file}`; its receipt is the existing native collector's `captures.json`, and its manifest is the map's `.captures.json`. Paths resolve relative to the pack, or relative to each native receipt for that receipt's map/image entries. PNG and PPM are supported; packed JPEG motion sheets cannot supply these measurements.

Collect cue-on and independently repeated cue-off images from the same held simulation frame, body pose and normal camera; suppress only the named cue/projectile, preserving the body, HUD, background and other scene contents. A pre-cue frame with a different body animation cannot be used as the control. Exclude body/HUD/name/stamp pixels from all footprint counts. Differences outside the effect region refuse the sample, and pixels differing between the two cue-off controls are discarded as background noise. The producer verifies the native collector's exact offline 3.0.0.24268 binding, map hash, fixture/frame membership, resolution and, for an uncropped capture, the held frame's stamp in its own pixels. It hashes the pack, map, manifests, receipts and images into provenance. Renderer and model-only samples remain unproven. This command reads evidence and launches no client.

Collect a sample with a capture pad carrying `#! conceal-cues a|b FIGHTER MOVE [WIDTHxHEIGHT]` (smashcraft:docs/commands/native.md): each held frame is grabbed shown, then with `-dev hide cues SLOT`, and the map records the frame's projected strike and hurt boxes, from the camera it drew, in the receipt's `screen` lines. Run the map twice; `bun scripts/classicScreenPack.ts --spec SPEC.json --out PACK.json` takes the first run's shown and hidden grabs and the second run's hidden grab for every frame, grows the region (strike and hurt boxes) and the body (hurt boxes) by the spec's `regionMargin` (a sample may carry its own, for an ultimate whose cue fills the screen) and `bodyMargin` pixels, moves the screen-space boxes into the `crop` image, and adds its `excluded` rectangles, plus the spec's optional `defender` box cut to the part right of each frame's region: the idle defender keeps animating through the cue-settle hold, so its edges differ between the shown and hidden grabs, while cue pixels over it inside the region still count. A spec names `excluded`, `crop`, `regionMargin`, `bodyMargin`, optionally `defender`, and `samples`, each with `move`, `role`, `version`, `source`, `fixture`, `manifest`, its two `runs` (receipt paths, relative to the spec) and optionally `regionMargin`. The calibration pads are smashcraft:ts/test/native/pads/454-screen: current-main Blademaster positives and the negatives, built from 0.0.192's source.
